Wealth Concentration, Part 1: What the Data Shows

Has wealth concentrated faster in the last ten years? At the very top, yes, and sharply. For the top 1% as a whole, hardly at all. Where the acceleration is, when it happened, and which assets carried it

The first of a series on why wealth has concentrated in so few hands. Using the Federal Reserve’s Distributional Financial Accounts, the Forbes billionaire lists and the World Inequality Database, this part establishes the facts: the US top 0.1% share rose from 12.6% to 15.0% in ten years while the rest of the top 1% lost share; billionaire wealth doubled relative to world GDP; two-thirds of the gain was listed shares; and the top 1% share is flat in most countries.

Wealth Concentration, Part 3: The Feedback Loops

Random growth alone concentrates wealth without limit; turnover holds it in check. A model fitted to fifteen years of billionaire lists shows a stable system until 2020, a wider spread of fortunes in 2020–22, and since 2023 a regime with no steady state

A model of wealth concentration at the top, built from the Forbes lists: multiplicative growth as the basic reinforcing loop, turnover as the balancing loop, why that combination produces a Pareto tail, a fixed-size simulation of the top 1,000 fortunes validated on 2011–2026, three regimes, projections under each, and what the model says about whether the process limits itself. With a causal loop diagram and an interactive simulation.
